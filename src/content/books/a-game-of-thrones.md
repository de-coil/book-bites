## A Game of Thrones Review

### A Book About Politics (And Headaches)

The novel opens with a Stark family who believe in honor. This is a mistake. Everyone else believes in scheming.

Ned Stark is asked to be Hand of the King. He accepts. This goes poorly.

Meanwhile, across the sea, dragons are being born. This is also significant.

---

### Plot, Politically Speaking

The narrative follows multiple families as they scheme:

- The Starks try to be honorable (it doesn't work)
- The Lannisters scheme constantly (it works too well)
- The Targaryens are in exile (for now)
- The Night's Watch guards a wall (something is beyond it)
- Everyone has a direwolf (except the ones who don't)

A tournament is held. Things go wrong. They often do.

---

### Characters, Mostly Doomed

The cast is extensive:

- **Ned Stark** – Honorable. This is a fatal flaw
- **Cersei Lannister** – Schemes. Has opinions about power
- **Jon Snow** – Knows nothing (allegedly). Joins the Night's Watch
- **Daenerys Targaryen** – In exile. Has dragons now
- **Tyrion Lannister** – Drinks and knows things. Survives through wit
- **Arya Stark** – Has a list. Very motivated

Character development occurs through betrayal and survival.

---

### Themes, Unfortunately

The book explores:

- the cost of honor in a dishonest world
- power as a corrupting influence
- whether family loyalty is worth dying for
- the importance of having a good lawyer

A political discussion lasts several chapters. It ends in death.

---

### Favourite Passage

> "Winter is coming."

This warning is repeated until it becomes a threat, then a promise, then a reality.

---

### Final Thoughts

This is a book about politics, betrayal, and dragons. It contains all three in equal measure.

Recommended for readers who enjoy:

- complex political intrigue
- characters who die unexpectedly
- moral ambiguity as a lifestyle
- dragons as endgame content

Overall rating: **8 out of 10 perfectly sharp ice thrones**.
