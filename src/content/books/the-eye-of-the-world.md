## The Eye of the World Review

### A Book About Prophecy (And Walking)

The novel opens with three boys from a small village who discover they're central to a prophecy. They leave home. They walk. A lot.

The Dark One is trying to break free and destroy the world. This is a recurring problem.

An Aes Sedai named Moiraine knows things. She tells some of them.

---

### Plot, Journey-Style

The narrative follows the group as they travel:

- They leave the Two Rivers (home)
- They are chased by Trollocs (bad)
- They discover their destinies (complicated)
- They travel to various places (many)
- They learn about the Wheel of Time (confusing)

A waygate is used. It involves traveling through patterns.

---

### Characters, Mostly Young

The cast is large and growing:

- **Rand** – Village boy. Turns out to be important. Very stressed
- **Mat** – Village boy. Gambles. Has bad luck (literally)
- **Perrin** – Village boy. Has wolf problems. Very serious
- **Moiraine** – Aes Sedai. Knows things. Manipulative
- **Egwene** – Village girl. Wants to learn. Very determined
- **Nynaeve** – Wisdom. Powerful. Angry (often)

Character development occurs through travel and revelation.

---

### Themes, Cyclically

The book explores:

- destiny versus choice
- the burden of being chosen
- whether the world can be saved (again)
- the importance of knowing one's history

A description of the Wheel of Time lasts several pages. It is somehow still interesting.

---

### Favourite Passage

> "The Wheel of Time turns, and Ages come and pass."

This is repeated until it becomes a mantra, then a warning, then a promise.

---

### Final Thoughts

This is a book about prophecy, travel, and destiny. It contains all three in equal measure.

Recommended for readers who enjoy:

- epic journeys with many stops
- complex prophecies and destinies
- characters discovering they're important
- a Dark One who won't stay sealed

Overall rating: **7 out of 10 perfectly balanced wheels of time**.
