## Project Hail Mary Review

### A Book That Starts With Amnesia (Conveniently)

The novel opens with a man waking up on a spaceship. He doesn't remember his name, his mission, or how to use the controls. This is presented as a minor inconvenience.

He discovers he's the last hope for humanity. The pressure is immense. He responds with jokes.

Meanwhile, a spider-like alien named Rocky is also trying to save his species. They communicate through math and music.

---

### Plot, Scientifically Speaking

The narrative follows Ryland as he solves problems:

- He figures out what his mission is (save Earth)
- He discovers an alien friend
- They work together to solve an extinction-level threat
- Science happens. Lots of science
- Things explode (sometimes)

A substance called astrophage is introduced. It eats stars. This is bad.

---

### Characters, Mostly Human (And One Not)

The cast is small but effective:

- **Ryland Grace** – Scientist. Good at problem-solving, bad at self-preservation
- **Rocky** – Alien. Communicates through musical notes. Excellent engineer
- **Eva Stratt** – Appears in flashbacks. Very intense. Makes difficult decisions
- **The Ship** – Has a computer voice. Occasionally unhelpful

Character development occurs through crisis management and cross-species friendship.

---

### Themes, Theoretically

The book explores:

- cooperation across species barriers
- the ethics of sacrificing individuals for the greater good
- whether sarcasm is a valid survival strategy
- the importance of problem-solving under pressure

A scientific explanation lasts several pages. It is somehow still interesting.

---

### Favourite Passage

> "Science is about finding out what's true. Not about what you wish were true."

This principle is applied repeatedly, often with explosive results.

---

### Final Thoughts

This is a book about science, friendship, and saving the world. It contains all three in equal measure.

Recommended for readers who enjoy:

- hard science fiction with humor
- unlikely friendships across species
- problem-solving as a plot device
- characters who joke in the face of extinction

Overall rating: **9 out of 10 perfectly calibrated spectrometers**.
