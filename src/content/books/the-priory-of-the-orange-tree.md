## The Priory of the Orange Tree Review

### A Book About Dragons (And Queens)

The novel opens with a world that believes dragons are extinct. They are wrong.

A queen named Sabran must produce an heir to protect her kingdom. A scholar named Ead discovers the truth about dragons.

The world is ending. Again.

---

### Plot, Draconically Speaking

The narrative follows multiple characters:

- Sabran rules the West (under pressure)
- Ead serves as Sabran's lady-in-waiting (secretly)
- They discover the truth about dragons
- Political intrigue unfolds (dangerous)
- A ancient enemy awakens (bad)

A priory is important. So is a tree.

---

### Characters, Queenly And Otherwise

The cast is varied:

- **Sabran** – Queen. Under pressure. Very determined
- **Ead** – Lady-in-waiting. Has secrets. Protective
- **Niklays** – Scholar. Studies dragons. Very curious
- **Tunoa** – Warrior. Seeks glory. Very skilled
- **The Nameless One** – Ancient enemy. Very old. Very angry

Character development occurs through revelation and duty.

---

### Themes, Draconically

The book explores:

- the nature of duty versus desire
- whether history can be trusted
- the importance of female leadership
- the cost of keeping secrets

A history of dragons lasts several pages. It is somehow still interesting.

---

### Favourite Passage

> "The dragon is not extinct. It merely sleeps."

This is repeated until it becomes a warning.

---

### Final Thoughts

This is a book about dragons, queens, and ancient secrets. It contains all three in equal measure.

Recommended for readers who enjoy:

- epic fantasy with strong female leads
- dragons as ancient threats
- political intrigue and duty
- worlds where history is wrong

Overall rating: **8 out of 10 perfectly ancient dragons**.
