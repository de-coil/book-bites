## Good Omens Review

### A Book About the End (With Humor)

The novel opens with the Antichrist being misplaced. This is inconvenient for everyone.

An angel named Aziraphale and a demon named Crowley have grown fond of Earth. They decide to stop the apocalypse. This is against their respective employers' wishes.

The world is ending on Saturday. They have until then.

---

### Plot, Apocalyptically Speaking

The narrative follows the duo as they scheme:

- They search for the Antichrist (misplaced)
- They try to prevent the Four Horsemen (difficult)
- They argue about the nature of good and evil
- They meet a witchfinder (confused)
- They attempt to save the world (against orders)

A bookshop is important. So is a record store.

---

### Characters, Celestial And Infernal

The cast is supernatural:

- **Aziraphale** – Angel. Likes books. Very polite
- **Crowley** – Demon. Likes cars. Very efficient
- **Adam Young** – Antichrist. Just wants to be a normal boy
- **Anathema Device** – Witch. Has prophecies (accurate)
- **Newton Pulsifer** – Witchfinder. Bad with technology
- **The Four Horsemen** – Apocalypse riders. Very motivated

Character development occurs through cooperation and argument.

---

### Themes, Eschatologically

The book explores:

- the nature of good versus evil
- whether humanity deserves saving
- the importance of free will
- the absurdity of cosmic bureaucracy

A theological discussion lasts several pages. It is somehow still funny.

---

### Favourite Passage

> "In the beginning, God created the heavens and the earth."

This is followed by a lengthy explanation of what went wrong.

---

### Final Thoughts

This is a book about the apocalypse, friendship, and humanity. It contains all three in equal measure.

Recommended for readers who enjoy:

- humor in serious situations
- unlikely friendships
- theological discussions with jokes
- the end of the world (with good intentions)

Overall rating: **9 out of 10 perfectly efficient ineffable plans**.
